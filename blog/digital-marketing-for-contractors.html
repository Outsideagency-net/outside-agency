Digital Marketing for Contractors and Home Service Businesses: The Complete Guide

By Outside Agency · November 9, 2026 · 5 min read

Digital marketing for contractors is not complicated, but the order you do it in matters. HVAC companies, plumbers, electricians, and roofers get pitched on everything from TikTok to billboards. Most of it can wait. A few things can't.

This guide walks through what works for home service businesses, roughly in the order we would do it, and what to skip until the basics are in place.

Start with your Google Business Profile

When someone searches "furnace repair near me," Google often shows a map with a short list of local businesses. That list comes from Google Business Profiles. If you only fix one thing this month, fix this.

Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and prominence. Relevance means your profile matches what people search for. Distance is how far you are from the searcher. Prominence is how well known you are, which Google says includes things like links to your business and how many reviews you have.

Google is also clear that "there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking." Anyone selling guaranteed map rankings is selling something Google says doesn't exist.

What you can do:

  • Verify your profile.
  • Pick the right primary category, like "HVAC contractor" or "Plumber."
  • Add your service area, hours, and phone number, and keep them accurate.
  • Upload real photos of your trucks, team, and finished jobs.

Reviews are your best salesperson

For contractors, reviews do a lot of the selling before you ever get a call. BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, which polled 1,002 US adults, found that 68% of consumers will only use a business with four or more stars. It also found that 74% look for reviews written in the last three months.

That second number is the one most contractors miss. A pile of great reviews from 2022 doesn't help much if nothing new has come in since. The fix is a habit, not a campaign: ask every happy customer for a review, ideally by text, right after the job is done.

Replying matters too. In the same survey, 80% of consumers said they are likely to use a business that responds to all of its reviews, and 50% said generic or templated replies put them off. Write a real reply. Mention the job. Thank them by name.

Local Services Ads: pay for leads, not clicks

Local Services Ads are the ads at the top of Google with the business name, rating, and a call button. For many home service trades, they are the most direct way to get phone calls from Google. You pay for valid leads instead of clicks.

Two things to know. First, a missed call during business hours still counts as a lead if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds, so answer your phone. Second, these ads are moving into Google Ads. Google started migrating select US home service advertisers, including plumbing, HVAC, electrical, and roofing, in August 2026. More businesses follow in late 2026, and the rest in 2027. Budgets and bidding settings change in the move, so review your campaign once it happens.

Google search ads: what home service leads cost

Regular search ads give you more control than Local Services Ads. You choose the services, the areas, and the pages people land on. They also cost more per lead in some trades. Here are the medians from LocaliQ's home services benchmarks, based on 3,211 US campaigns from April 2024 to March 2025:

Look at roofing. Clicks cost about the same as plumbing, but the conversion rate is less than half, so each lead costs far more. That doesn't mean ads don't work for roofers. A roof is a big job, so a $228 lead can still be very profitable. It does mean roofing campaigns need tight targeting and strong landing pages to pay off.

Your website's only job is to get the call

A contractor website doesn't need to win design awards. It needs to answer three questions fast: what do you do, where do you do it, and how do I reach you. If a visitor on a phone can't find your number without scrolling, fix that first.

Give each main service its own page. Someone searching for "tankless water heater install" should land on a page about tankless water heaters, with photos of your work, a few reviews, and a way to call or book. Pages like that can help with Google rankings, and they help with ads too, because Google judges ads partly on how well the landing page matches what the person expected.

What can wait

Here is where we may disagree with other agencies, including on services we sell. Social media ads, connected TV, and digital billboards can all work for home service businesses. They work best once the basics are in place. If your Google profile is half finished, your reviews are old, and nobody answers the phone after 4 p.m., more awareness just sends more people to a weak first impression.

Once the foundation is solid, these channels are good at keeping your name in front of homeowners before they need you, so they think of you when the AC quits in July.

The order we recommend

  1. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile.
  2. Set up a simple, steady way to ask for reviews, and reply to every one.
  3. Make sure every call gets answered, or at least returned quickly.
  4. Build a clear website with one page per main service.
  5. Run Local Services Ads if your trade is eligible.
  6. Add Google search ads for the services and areas you want to grow.
  7. Track every call and form so you know your real cost per job.
  8. Then add social, video, and other awareness channels.

Skipping ahead is tempting, especially when a slick sales pitch shows up. Get the basics right first, and every channel you add later has something solid to send people to.
